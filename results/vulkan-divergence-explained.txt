The last 5 Vulkan divergences are near-ties, not errors
=======================================================

After the graph_optimize race was fixed, the trace gate went from 1/16
reproducing the CUDA oracle to 11/16, and this repo recorded the remaining
5 as "real divergences, unexplained". They are now explained, and the answer
is that they are not errors.

WHERE THEY OCCUR
----------------
Every one lands on a token where the model is almost exactly undecided:

  prompt                    tok    CUDA           Vulkan          top1-top2 gap
  code/c-matmul              78    ' &&'          ';'                  0.0651
  code/cuda-reduce           96    ' blockDim'    ' warp'              0.0024
  prose/coastal-town         41    ' anticipation' ' an'               0.0251
  prose/history-canal        29    ' sever'       ' drastically'       0.0143
  prose/travel-highlands     37    ' Season'      ' Time'              0.0263

A gap of 0.065 nats is a probability ratio of 1.067 -- a 52:48 coin flip.
0.0024 nats is 1.002:1.

HOW OFTEN THE MODEL IS THAT UNDECIDED
--------------------------------------
Across all 2048 scored tokens in the oracle:

  median top1-top2 gap    4.712 nats      (the model is usually certain)
  gap <= 0.0050            0.05% of tokens
  gap <= 0.0250            0.49%
  gap <= 0.0651            1.17%

So a near-tie is rare per token but common per trace: at 1.17%, the chance
that a 128-token trace contains at least one is **78%**, and the expected
number of diverging traces out of 16 is **12.5** if every tie flipped.

**We observe 5.** The Vulkan kernels therefore agree with CUDA at most
near-ties and diverge at a minority of them. The divergence rate is not
merely explainable -- it is well below what independent tie-breaking would
produce.

THE STRONGER EVIDENCE: CPU DIVERGES AT THE SAME PLACES
-------------------------------------------------------
Comparing both non-CUDA backends against the same oracle, and measuring the
drift BEFORE the first divergence (after it, everything downstream differs,
so post-divergence drift measures nothing):

  backend                identical   worst pre-divergence |dlogprob|
  Vulkan (deterministic)   11/16                 0.1312
  CPU (NEON)               13/16                 0.0584

  diverging trace          Vulkan             CPU
  prose/coastal-town       tok 41, 0.0567     tok 41, 0.0573
  prose/history-canal      tok 29, 0.0423     tok 29, 0.0423
  code/c-matmul            tok 78, 0.0266     --
  code/cuda-reduce         tok 96, 0.0345     --
  prose/travel-highlands   tok 37, 0.0057     --
  prose/philosophy-memory  --                 tok  6, 0.0512

**Two prompts flip on BOTH backends, at the same token, with the same
pre-divergence drift to three decimals.** Those points are a property of the
model, not of any backend: at token 41 of coastal-town the model is
genuinely undecided and any kernel with a different summation order lands on
the other side.

WHAT THIS SAYS ABOUT THE GATE
------------------------------
The gate reports `max|dlogprob|` over the whole trace and fails at 0.05. On
a diverged trace that figure is dominated by POST-divergence drift (up to
2.18), which measures nothing -- once the token differs, the two runs are
generating different text and their logprobs are not comparable.

The meaningful number is the pre-divergence drift, and there Vulkan is
0.131 worst-case against CPU's 0.058. Same order of magnitude, both
consistent with quantisation and reduction-order noise.

CONCLUSION
----------
Vulkan is a numerically ordinary backend. The remaining 5 divergences are
float reassociation at coin-flip tokens, the same phenomenon as the CPU
backend's 3, and two of them are literally the same tokens. There is no
outstanding Vulkan correctness defect in this data.

What would still be worth doing: report pre-divergence drift in the gate
rather than whole-trace max, so the number tracks numerical agreement rather
than tie density. As it stands a backend can be flawless and still "fail"
because the model was undecided at token 41 of a prose prompt.
